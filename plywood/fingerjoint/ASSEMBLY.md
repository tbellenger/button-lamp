## How the joints work
- **Back, top, slope and front** each carry tabs on both ends: 24 mm long, or 12 mm on the front. The tabs drop into open notches cut in the edges of the two sides, and the tab ends finish flush with the outside faces of the sides.
- **Fit:** each notch is 0.30 mm longer than its tab and 0.15 mm deeper than the sheet is thick, which is SendCutSend's slip fit. Every inside corner has a Ø2.2 mm dogbone, so the square corners seat fully.
- **Back to top** is a 90° finger joint: the back has 3 tabs (28 mm) that rise into 3 notches in the top's back edge.
- **Top/slope crease (144°) and slope/front crease (126°)** have no joint, because square laser edges can't meet at those angles. Each panel is held only by its side tabs and stops 0.15 mm short of its neighbour. That leaves a small V groove on the outside along each crease: about 2.0 mm at the top crease and about 2.7 mm at the front crease. Fill them with wood filler (or glue mixed with sanding dust) and sand, or leave them as a shadow line.

## What's glued and what's screwed
- **Glued (PVA wood glue):** every tab joint, side to back, top, slope and front, plus the back-to-top fingers. The 6 printed blocks are glued to the walls.
- **Screwed:** the bottom tray (6 screws up into the blocks) and the spiral cage (4 × M3 through the top, with the backing plate). v2 has no switch panel to screw on: the controls mount straight in the control slope (the fader takes 2 × M3 from below). With the as-ordered slope, the printed panel or the birch retrofit faceplate screws on with 4 × M3.

## Order of assembly
1. **Dry fit everything first.** It's a slip fit, so the parts should push together by hand.
   - If a tab is tight (ply thickness varies), sand its faces lightly rather than the notches.
   - Before you glue, mark the faces:
     - **Back:** the DXF is drawn from inside. Seen from behind, the USB-B window is at the left end and the USB-C slot is at the right.
     - **Top:** the DXF is drawn from above. The 10 mm wire hole sits near the back edge, just right of centre as seen from the front.
     - **Sides:** both are the same part. Flip one over for the other side.
2. Lay one side flat, inside face up.
3. Brush glue into its notches and on the tabs. Stand the back, front and slope in place.
4. Lower the top onto the back's three tabs and into its side notches.
5. Glue the second side on top, guiding all 8 tabs into its notches.
6. Drop the bottom tray in (no glue, no screws) to use as a squaring jig.
   - It has 0.5 mm clearance all round, so the box is square when the tray drops in evenly.
   - Clamp or tape the sides together and wipe off squeeze-out.
   - Let it cure for about 1 hour, or overnight before any stress.
7. **Blocks:** with the tray still sitting in place and the box upright on a flat bench, glue the 6 printed blocks to the walls.
   - Put glue on the wall faces only, never on the tray.
   - Press each block down onto the tray so it sits exactly at tray height.
   - Positions: the 4 corners plus the middle of each side, matching the tray's 6 screw holes.
   - Once cured, take the tray out.
8. Sand the tab ends flush, then fill and sand the two crease grooves if you want a clean line. Then do the finishing (see `plywood/PLAN.md` §8).
9. Fit the electronics to the tray as before. Screw the tray up into the blocks and bolt the cage to the top. v2: fit the controls to the control slope **before step 2** is fine too, but it's easier after the glue-up because the slope is held square; leave the harness long (250 mm).
